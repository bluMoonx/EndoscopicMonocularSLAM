02The project in one breath

In plain words

During a colonoscopy, doctors miss about one in four precancerous growths — often because some patch of the colon wall simply never got looked at. Argus watches the video from a standard, unmodified endoscope and builds, from that video alone, a 3D map of the colon plus a coverage map: which areas were inspected, and which are blind spots the camera never saw. No new hardware, no extra sensors — just software on the video feed.

The technical version

A fully monocular endoscopic mapping pipeline: per-frame depth from a domain-fine-tuned Depth-Anything-V2, 6-DOF camera poses from a from-scratch dense photometric (direct) visual odometry, dense surface reconstruction by TSDF volumetric fusion, and per-patch coverage counting for blind-spot detection. Every stage is validated independently against the C3VD phantom's ground-truth depth and 6-DOF poses via a staged ablation.

The one-sentence pitch: “I built a fully monocular endoscopy mapping system, used a controlled ablation to prove the pre-trained depth network was the bottleneck, fine-tuned it for the endoscopy domain with leave-one-out validation, and that single fix carried the whole system from 74% to 98.6% agreement with ground truth.”

03The story — how it became this

Argus didn't start here. The arc below is the science: every pivot was forced by an honest measurement. Tell it in this order and judges see a real investigation, not a demo.

  1. Act I · the glare hypothesis

    “Removing glare will fix endoscopic SLAM” — tested, and mostly wrong

    Rigorous multi-condition runs showed feature-based ORB-SLAM3 is already glare-robust (its RANSAC outlier rejection discards glare corrupted points), and naive inpainting actually hurts by destroying trackable texture. AI inpainting (LaMa) recovers to baseline but never beats it. An honest negative result with a mechanism.

  2. Act I½ · the paradigm insight

    Glare matters for direct methods — so we built one

    Brightness-based (direct/photometric) tracking is glare-sensitive where feature-based isn't: removal gave 10–17% tracking-residual improvement scaling with glare severity. To show it at trajectory level we built our own dense direct visual odometry from scratch — which validated at 0.38 mm ATE against C3VD ground truth and became the engine of everything after.

  3. Act II · the pivot

    The blind-spot map becomes the headline

    With accurate tracking in hand, the old stretch goal — mapping what the camera never inspected — became the clinically meaningful, buildable centerpiece. Validated first with ground-truth depth: 97.8–99.6% agreement with true blind spots across five sequences.

  4. Act III · going fully monocular

    Replace every ground-truth input — and find the bottleneck

    A real scope has no depth sensor. Swapping in off-the-shelf learned depth broke the system (74.5% mean agreement, some sequences at chance). The ablation ladder (§05) proved depth — not tracking — was the sole bottleneck.

  5. Act IV · the fix

    Domain fine-tuning, validated leave-one-out

    Fine-tuned the depth network on C3VD colon data with strict leave-one-sequence-out cross-validation. Held-out depth accuracy went 68%→98%; the full monocular system recovered to 98.6% mean blind-spot agreement, 0.91 mm mean tracking error, and 1.3–2.4 mm reconstruction accuracy.

04The system, stage by stage

Four stages, each with its plain and technical version. Video goes in one end; a 3D colon with blind spots highlighted comes out the other.

Stage 1 — Learned depth (one frame → distance at every pixel)

In plain words

A single photo has no depth — but a neural network that has seen millions of images has learned what shapes make what pictures, so it can estimate how far away every pixel is. We took a big pre-trained network and gave it a short extra course on colon images so it works in the gut.

The technical version

Depth-Anything-V2 (a pre-trained vision transformer) outputs affine-invariant inverse depth ("disparity"): correct shape, unknown scale/shift — monocular depth is provably scale-ambiguous, so one global scale per sequence is recovered, the standard protocol (same reason monocular SLAM evaluation uses Sim(3) alignment). Fine-tuned on C3VD RGB→metric-depth pairs: 40 epochs, AdamW 1e-5, loss = L1 + gradient-matching + scale-invariant log (SiLog). Evaluated leave-one-sequence-out: five models, each tested only on the sequence it never saw.

Stage 2 — Direct visual odometry (frames → camera path)

In plain words

The tracker figures out how the camera moved between two frames by asking: “what tiny 3D motion would make frame A line up with frame B, pixel for pixel?” Chain those little motions together and you get the camera's whole path through the colon.

The technical version

Dense direct VO, written from scratch: minimizes the photometric residual over SE(3) via inverse-compositional Gauss–Newton on a 4-level image pyramid, warping with per-pixel depth; Huber robust weighting optional. Frame-to-frame motions are composed into a trajectory (TUM format). Fully monocular ATE: 0.17–1.59 mm across the five sequences (evo, Sim(3)-aligned).

Stage 3 — TSDF fusion (many noisy depth maps → one clean 3D mesh)

In plain words

Each frame's depth is a bit noisy. Instead of piling up noisy points, we carve space into tiny 0.6 mm cubes and let every frame vote on where the colon wall is. Hundreds of votes average the noise away — the same reason averaging many shaky measurements gives one steady answer. The result is a smooth 3D surface, more accurate than any single frame that built it.

The technical version

TSDF = Truncated Signed Distance Function: each voxel stores a running average of its signed distance to the surface (negative inside tissue, positive in the lumen). Every frame's depth map updates voxels along its rays; marching cubes extracts the zero-crossing as a triangle mesh (the KinectFusion algorithm; Open3D implementation, 0.6 mm voxels). Median surface error vs the ground-truth mesh: 1.3–2.4 mm (Sim(3)+ICP-aligned Chamfer distance).

Stage 4 — Coverage & blind spots (mesh + path → what was never seen)

In plain words

Now we know the shape of the colon and where the camera was pointing at every moment. For each little patch of wall, count how many times a camera actually saw it. Patches seen fewer than three times get flagged red: blind spots — go back and look.

The technical version

Each surface patch is projected into every camera pose; it counts as seen if in-frustum, front-facing, and not occluded (occlusion tolerance scaled to the depth model's measured accuracy band — one fixed setting for every sequence and every condition, never tuned per case). Patches with view-count < 3 are blind. Validated against blind spots computed from ground-truth depth+poses on the same surface.

05The experiment: the ablation ladder

In plain words

The system estimates two things: depth and camera position. When the output was bad, which one was to blame? Answer like a scientist: swap each estimated input for the perfect (ground-truth) version, one at a time, and see which swap fixes it. That's a controlled experiment on software.

The technical version

Coverage is computed on the ground-truth surface under six input conditions; each stage's blind-spot map is scored against Stage A (the reference). One fixed configuration throughout — no per-sequence tuning.

07Three traps we caught — judge catnip

These are the moments that separate an investigation from a demo. Learn all three; each has a “how we caught it” story.

Trap 1 · the alignment roll

A 75° error that wasn't real

Plain: Two sequences looked terrible despite sub-millimeter tracking. The cameras move nearly straight down the colon — and if you line up two straight paths using only positions, nothing pins down the twist around the path. Our comparison had the camera rolled 75°, looking at the wrong wall. The tracker was never wrong; the grading was.

Technical: Position-only Umeyama/Sim(3) alignment leaves the roll about a near-straight trajectory's motion axis unconstrained. Diagnosis: the per-frame orientation offset R_gt·R_voᵀ had 1–2° dispersion — one global rotation separated the trajectories, not drift. Fix: estimate the global rotation from orientations (mean offset), scale from positions. Agreement: 64% → 97.8%, tracker untouched.

Trap 2 · the scale question

“Isn't using ground truth for scale cheating?”

Plain: One camera can't tell a big colon far away from a small colon up close — the images are identical. So every monocular system is allowed one global “how big is this?” calibration; ours uses one number per video. Everything per-frame is prediction. And after fine-tuning, three of five sequences didn't even need it — the network learned the scope's true scale.

Technical: Monocular reconstruction is defined up to scale; per-sequence global affine recovery (and Sim(3)-aligned ATE) is the standard evaluation protocol in the SLAM literature, not a concession.

Trap 3 · metric honesty

Reporting the flattering number and the strict one

Plain: “98.6% agreement” sounds great partly because most of the wall was seen — agreeing on “seen” is easy. So we also report the hard numbers: can it pinpoint the exact blind vertices (only 20%/10% — weak) and can it point to the right area within 5 mm (65%/53% — useful, since the clinical action is “re-inspect this area”). Both were defined before comparing, both get reported.

Technical: With a rare positive class (blind ≈ 1–6%), accuracy-style agreement is inflated and IoU is punishing; we report agreement, coverage correlation, vertex-level recall/precision, and region-level (5 mm tolerance) recall/precision — all defined once, applied uniformly to baseline and fine-tuned systems alike.

08Honest limitations

  • Phantom validation. All ground-truthed results are on the C3VD silicone colon phantom. Real tissue deforms, has fluids and stronger glare — in-vivo validation is future work, and you say so before anyone asks.
  • Region-level, not point-level, localization. The system reliably maps coverage and points at under-inspected areas; per-millimeter pinpointing is not clinical-grade yet.
  • Short-motion scale drift. One sequence (trans-t1-a, only 28 mm of camera travel) sits at the tracker's noise floor, so its reconstruction drifts in scale — a characterized failure with a mechanism, reported, not hidden.
  • Domain data. Leave-one-out shows the depth fine-tune generalizes across sequences; generalizing across patients and scope models would need broader training data.
Why limitations are strength

Every limitation above comes with its mechanism. “I know exactly where it breaks and why” is the most credible sentence a young researcher can say.

10The data

C3VD — the ground-truth backbone

The Colonoscopy 3D Video Dataset: video of a high-fidelity silicone colon phantom recorded with a real clinical endoscope, with true per-pixel depth and true 6-DOF camera poses for every frame (from the phantom's known geometry and a robotic rig). Five sequences used (cecum, descending, three transverse), spanning 61–276 frames. This is what makes every claim checkable: depth accuracy, tracking ATE, mesh Chamfer, and blind-spot agreement are all graded against it. Frames are fisheye — undistorted to pinhole via the Scaramuzza/OcamCalib model before use.

Hamlyn — the in-vivo glare testbed (Act I)

Real in-body endoscopic video, wet and glare-heavy — where the glare study ran. It ships stereo-derived depth but no trajectory ground truth (a fact we verified early, killing the original evaluation plan and forcing the better one).

11The fair & the paperwork

The path is regional → state → international: GSDSEF → CSEF → ISEF. You need your fair's review committee to approve the plan before experimenting. Because the project uses public, pre-recorded, de-identified datasets — no live patients or animals — the review is normally the light kind.

12Honest verdict

The build phase delivered: a complete, validated, honestly-characterized system — with a genuinely competitive story for state and a real shot beyond.

What you have that most projects don't: (1) an end-to-end system, not a single technique; (2) a controlled ablation that found a bottleneck and a targeted fix that closed it, validated leave-one-out; (3) negative results and failure cases reported with mechanisms; (4) every number graded against real ground truth.

What decides how far it goes now: the write-up, the poster, and above all the interview — whether you can walk a judge down the ablation ladder, explain TSDF fusion with the voting analogy, and own the limitations before they're raised. The system is done; the remaining work is making its story yours.

13Glossary

Every term here is fair game in the interview.

Monocular
One camera, one lens. Harder than stereo — one eye can't judge absolute distance, so scale must be recovered separately.
SLAM / VO
Working out where the camera is (and what the space looks like) from video alone. Visual odometry (VO) is the tracking half: chaining frame-to-frame motions into a path.
Direct / photometric
Tracking by matching raw pixel brightness between frames (vs feature-based, which tracks distinctive landmark points). Our tracker is direct.
Gauss–Newton
The iterative math that finds the camera motion minimizing the pixel mismatch — guess, measure the error's slope, step, repeat.
Disparity / inverse depth
What the depth network outputs: 1/distance. Big = near. Correct in shape but unknown in scale.
Affine / scale ambiguity
Monocular depth (and monocular SLAM) is only defined up to a scale (and shift). One global correction per sequence is the standard, legitimate protocol.
Fine-tuning
Continuing a pre-trained network's training briefly on your domain's data so it adapts — days of compute, not months, because it starts from knowledge, not zero.
LOSO
Leave-One-Sequence-Out cross-validation: to test on sequence X, train only on the others. Five folds = five models. Kills the “did it just memorize?” objection.
TSDF
Truncated Signed Distance Function — a voxel grid where each cell stores its distance to the surface. Frames vote; noise averages out; marching cubes extracts the mesh. (The KinectFusion method.)
ATE (RMSE)
Absolute Trajectory Error — distance between estimated and true camera path after alignment. Our fully-monocular mean: 0.91 mm.
Sim(3) / Umeyama
The standard way to line up two trajectories: one global rotation + translation + scale. Position-only fitting has a blind spot on straight paths (Trap 1).
Chamfer distance
Average nearest-point distance between two surfaces, both directions — the mesh accuracy score.
δ<1.25
Standard depth metric: fraction of pixels within 25% of true depth. Ours went 68% → 98% with fine-tuning.
Ablation
Swapping/removing parts one at a time to measure what each contributes — the project's core experimental design.
Occlusion test
A wall patch only counts as “seen” if nothing sat between it and the camera. Tolerance scaled to the depth model's measured accuracy, fixed once for all conditions.
Coverage / blind spot
How many camera views actually saw each patch of the colon wall; fewer than 3 = blind spot, flagged for re-inspection.
Specular / glare
Bright reflections of the scope's own light off wet tissue. Act I's villain; matters for direct tracking, largely not for feature-based.
RANSAC
An outlier-rejection method: repeatedly fit on random subsets, keep the fit most points agree with. Why ORB-SLAM3 shrugs off glare.
Ground truth
The answer key (true depth, true poses) — C3VD has both; used only for grading and the one-per-sequence scale, never as a runtime input to the final system.
